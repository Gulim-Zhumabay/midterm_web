The Name of the Rose


Author: Umberto Eco
Year: 1980
Genre: Historical novel, detective (murder mystery), philosophical novel.


Core plot overview:
The story takes place in 1327 in a Benedictine abbey in northern Italy. A Franciscan friar, William of Baskerville, arrives with his young helper Adso. They come to prepare a meeting between church groups who are arguing about whether Jesus and the church should be poor.
When they arrive, a monk has already died in a strange way. More monks die one after another, and the abbot asks William to find the killer. William studies the clues and learns that the deaths are connected to the abbey’s secret library. It is built like a maze, and only the librarian may enter.
The deaths are linked to a forbidden book that some monks want to keep hidden. The blind old monk Jorge hides it and poisons its pages, so that anyone who reads it dies. He believes that laughter and this book could destroy faith. In the end, the library burns down, and the book is lost.
During this time, the inquisitor Bernard Gui arrives and accuses some monks of heresy, which is believing things the church forbids. Adso, now an old man, tells the story many years later.
