Martin Eden


Author: Jack London
Year: 1909
Genre: Novel, semi-autobiographical fiction, social and philosophical novel


Core plot overview:
Martin Eden is a young, poor sailor in Oakland, California. He meets Ruth Morse, a girl from a rich, educated family, and falls in love with her. He feels that he is not good enough for her, so he decides to educate himself. He reads many books and studies hard, and he learns to write.
He wants to become a famous writer so he can marry Ruth. For a long time, magazines reject all his work. He lives in poverty and works very hard. Ruth’s family looks down on him, and in the end Ruth leaves him.
Later, Martin becomes famous and rich. People who ignored him now praise him. He sees that they like his fame and not the real him. He loses his love for Ruth, and he loses his belief in people and in his own success. He feels lonely and empty, and the book ends sadly with his death.
The main idea of the book is the danger of selfish individualism, and how success can feel empty if it does not bring real meaning.
